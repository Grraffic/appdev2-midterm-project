

**Unified Ordering System: A Digital QR-Based School Uniform Inventory and Event Merchandise Manual Payment Validation at La Verdad Christian School and College, Apalit**


**CHAPTER I**

**INTRODUCTION**

La Verdad (LV) is a private, non-profit, and non-sectarian educational institution in the Philippines. It is the country’s first private school to offer full scholarship programs, which include free tuition, waived miscellaneous fees, free school uniforms, and free lunches to worthy students (Ferrera, 2015). The institution was established in 1998 and initially offered preschool, elementary, and high school education, but it now offers a college education.

The core objective of the school is to provide educational assistance to poor but deserving students across the country and enable them to acquire quality education without cost. LV seeks to alleviate poverty and offers opportunities to poor but determined students to pursue higher education and fulfill their dreams with God’s help and mercy (*La Verdad Christian College to Educate Poor but Deserving Students*, n.d.).

As the La Verdad significantly expanded its commitment to providing free education, it substantially increased its student population. Aligned with the institution’s core objective of free education, one of its dedications is to provide students with free school uniforms. However, the student ordering process of the school uniforms poses issues and inefficiencies that impact the students.

**1-A. Background of the Study**

The students in LV currently face issues and inefficiencies in the ordering experience when requesting free school uniforms. The current process of requesting free school uniforms requires filling out an order form, a paper-based receipt that serves as a claim coupon for the ordered school uniforms. One of the other problems encountered by the students is the loss of paper-based receipts. This would require the Finance Department to check its copy of the paper-based record to verify order claims. In addition, students encounter inefficiencies such as delays in tracking the availability of school uniforms. This is due to the lack of inventory stock visibility on school uniforms. This forces the students to regularly inquire and follow up on their ordered products if the inventories are replenished. The lack of a streamlined ordering system with inventory tracking of the Finance Department results in the inefficiency of regularly going to the stockroom to inquire about the pending unclaimed uniforms due to product unavailability. Thus, students cannot track their uniform order availability or receive updates once the products are restocked.

In parallel to the free school uniform ordering system, students also participate in ordering other event merchandise. This event merchandise includes the Foundation Week Shirt managed by the Prefect of Student Affairs and Services (PSAS), available from basic to higher education. Event merchandise is also managed by the student organizations across the different departments in higher education.  The merchandise includes the program shirt of each organization, and sometimes may include other products, such as tote bags, notebooks, and other items, depending on the organization’s posted products. These event merchandise items are pre-ordered items, which need payment first before processing the orders. Validating the payment for the pre-ordered items will ensure that the students will receive the pre-ordered products that they have paid for.

The inconsistencies in the ordering systems employed by each organization and department force the student to navigate different ordering systems across various departments. The students use different ordering procedures for different departments or organizations, and will need to check each to track their order status manually. The lack of a unified platform in the ordering system inconveniences students with separate order transactions across the free school uniforms and paid event merchandise.

The challenges in the student ordering process highlight the critical need for a unified digital solution streamlining the ordering system of the school uniforms and the event merchandise. Due to the challenges and inefficiencies encountered by the students, they are burdened with unnecessary tasks that distract them from the primary focus, their academic pursuits. To address these challenges and inefficiencies, this research study proposes the development of the “Unified Ordering System: A Digital QR-Based School Uniform Inventory and Event Merchandise Manual Payment Validation at La Verdad Christian School and College, Apalit”.

Resolving the Finance Department’s lack of real-time inventory tracking will directly solve the issue of students regularly checking the inventory availability in claiming. The researchers study the principles of efficient inventory management and recognize that the potential of technological integration in inventory management has been applied in various educational institutions. Teves (2024) conducted a study and integrated QR codes as a means of tracking and monitoring resources. Their study highlights the role of the QR-based system in reducing resource loss and improving access to school-owned items. Similarly, Yaqin et al. (2024) at the Faculty of Health, Nurul Jadid University in Indonesia, implemented a QR code-based inventory system to track the laboratory equipment. Their study has successfully streamlined inventory management and improved the accuracy and transparency of consumable materials in tracking laboratory equipment.

Given the past studies, the proposed system not only aligns with best practices in inventory management and ordering systems but also prioritizes student experience at La Verdad Christian School and College, Apalit. The proposed system ensures seamless order placement and provides a real-time order tracking system. The proposed system enables manual payment validation and centralizes access to the school uniform requests and merchandise orders. By leveraging the unified platform with QR code technology for inventory management and manual payment validation for the event merchandise ordering system, inefficiencies such as a fragmented student ordering system, loss of paper-based receipt, and inefficient order tracking are addressed.

**Finance Department School Uniform Ordering Process**

The Finance Department of La Verdad Apalit Campus also manages the ordering process, transactions, and distribution of free school uniforms. The section below outlines the components of their current ordering process for the school uniform and the entities present in their process.

**Paper-based Order Receipt.** The Finance Department uses a paper-based order receipt to record school uniform order transactions made by the students. This tool is the department’s main instrument for collecting and documenting the students’ school uniform orders. There are two copies of the order form receipts filled out by the student, one for the student and one for the Finance Department records. The student copy serves as the claiming stub for the school uniform orders.

**Excel Spreadsheet.** The Finance Department utilizes this technology to check the previous order transactions made by the students. The order transaction records are the basis for checking the eligibility of the student filing for the school uniform order request. If an order was made in the past three consecutive academic years, the student will be ineligible to file for a school uniform.

**Finance Staff.** The Finance Department Staff manages the ordering process and distribution of the free school uniform. They facilitate the ordering of school uniforms and regularly check the physical inventory for school uniform availability. Once a student or parent fills out the order form, if the school uniform is available, the finance staff distributes the uniforms.

**Chancellor.** The Chancellor, Dr. Sharene Labung, is the main person responsible for approving or rejecting the student’s new school uniform order application. The Chancellor checks if the student’s reason for applying for the new school uniform order is valid or not. The approval of the new school uniform order request allows the student to be eligible to apply for a new school uniform.

**Students or Parents. ** The students are the consumers of the free school uniform provided by the institution. The parents or students fill out two copies of the order form, and one copy remains in their possession as a receipt of their order transaction, allowing them to claim the school uniform orders. The order receipt will serve as their order transaction stub to claim the orders they have placed.

***Footnote:** A student is eligible for a free uniform if they have not submitted a free uniform order within the past three consecutive academic years.*

**1-B.2 Proposed System**

To address challenges and inefficiencies being encountered by the students in the order processing of different products and manual inventory updates. The proposed system aims to centralize and unify the ordering system of school uniforms and event merchandise, such as Foundation Week Shirts and other merchandise pre-ordered by the college student organizations. The integration of innovative technologies, such as the use of QR codes in order claiming and real-time inventory updates, and the manual payment validation will address the challenges and issues in the lack of student order claiming. The implementation of QR-based inventory management into the Finance Department will help lessen the administrative workload of the Finance Staff in manually adjusting the inventory count. The students will be able to track their order details across different departments with ease, such as the item availability in the school uniform and order status in the event merchandise at La Verdad Christian School and College, Apalit.

**System Work Environment**

The proposed system operates a web-based platform that is accessible across different technologies, such as mobile phones and desktops, with ease through the internet. It provides a centralized portal with a department-specific dashboard for managing the ordering process of students across various departments and organizations. With integrated QR-based inventory management in school uniform and order payment validation equipped with role-based access, it streamlines order processing across various departments. This ensures that the students’ order transactions, the PSAS, and the college student organization order management, with payment validation and the Finance Department’s ordering and inventory management, are seamless and centralized.

**2.3.1.1 Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT)**

The Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) was proposed by Venkatesh et al. (2003) to examine factors influencing the acceptance and usage of an integrated technological platform. It highlights that the actual use of technology is determined by behavioral intention. The perceived likelihood of adopting the technology is dependent on the direct effect of four key constructs, namely performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions. The effect of predictors is moderated by age, gender, experience, and voluntariness of use (Venkatesh et al., 2003). The following are the four main factors:

* **Performance Expectancy:** It is the belief that technology will improve job performance and the strongest predictor of use intention and is significant in both voluntary and mandatory settings (Zhou, Lu & Wang, 2010; Venkatesh, Thong & Xu, 2016\)  
* **Effort Expectancy:** It is the perceived ease of use of the system. These factors become nonsignificant after extended usage of technology (Gupta, Dasgupta & Gupta, 2008; Chauhan & Jaiswal, 2016\)  
* **Social Influence:** It is the perception of others’ opinions about using the system. This factor has a big impact when technology utilization is required. In a mandated setting, people may employ technology to meet compliance requirements rather than for personal reasons (Venkatesh & Davis, 2000).  
* **Facilitating Conditions:** It is the belief that support exists in the organization’s and technical infrastructure. This factor implies that facilitating conditions directly impact actual usage behavior.

**2.3.1.3.2 Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) model**

The EOQ model minimizes wastage through the avoidance of overstocking and understocking and guarantees a timely supply of products through the right amount of stock to be replenished at any point in time.

The use of the Quota Sampling technique also further guarantees that each sample aligns with the relative size and importance of each educational segment, facilitating accuracy in understanding the needs and expectations of the user. 

**Purposive Sampling.** This study also utilizes the use of the purposive sampling technique to identify the stakeholders within the institution that are directly involved in the management of the proposed system. The following stakeholders are the key personnel, specifically from the PSAS Department, Finance Department, and various student organizations such as JSWAP, AIM, JPIA, and the Broadcasting Society. The involvement of these stakeholders in the implementation of the system benefits the success of the proposed system with their inclusion in interviews and system evaluations. 

**Modified Approach: Proxy Respondent Design.** This approach is employed in the study to consider the particular situations and cases that the prekindergarten and elementary students may encounter in the proposed system. The student respondents from prekindergarten and elementary may have limited access to mobile devices or web-based platforms. In this design, the parents or guardians of the said students act as the proxy respondents. These parents serve as the primary system users on behalf of the students, interacting with the proposed system to engage in tasks such as placing orders, tracking their orders, browsing event-based merchandise, and claiming their ordered products.

This design is essential in terms of acknowledging the reality of multi-users of the proposed system: the students, who are the primary beneficiaries, and the parents, as the system operators, who interact with the system on their behalf. Upon this, the researchers implement the use of survey questionnaires to gather insights from both the parents and students. The questions for the parents who act as the proxy respondents will focus on: 

1. Operational Experiences with the system, particularly in the use of information access, interface simplification, and QR scanning functionality.

2. Student-centered concerns include the claiming process, communication with the departments of the institution, and satisfaction with the quality of the products they ordered. 

By incorporating a proxy-respondent design, both the operational effectiveness of the proposed system and the user satisfaction are ensured in the dual approach design used in this research.  This approach helps provide an in-depth understanding of the existing challenges and user expectations related to uniform and event-based merchandise management. The use of these evaluations and user-centered assessment helps the study further evaluate and validate the proposed system's functionality while also analyzing the preferences of both parents and students.

**3.3 Instruments of the Study**

**Interviews.** Structured and semi-structured interviews will be conducted with the stakeholders facilitating the ordering system of students, such as the Finance Department staff, PSAS personnel, and student organization officers. The structured interview focused on acquiring feedback from the department and student organizations,  and students' practices and behaviors in their order transactions involving uniforms and event-based merchandise. This includes analyzing the student’s ordering experience and processes regarding inefficiencies in the ordering workflow process, transaction validations, and inventory availability on school uniforms. Furthermore, the semi-structured interviews will be conducted with stakeholders for more open-ended discussions, allowing them to provide unique insights into their ordering process of the event-based merchandise and school uniform orders and transactions.

These interviews aimed to gather qualitative insights on the current ordering processes, the students' pain points on the ordering system, and the specific requirements of the proposed system. This will help the researchers identify system expectations and usability needs. The responses will be gathered from these sessions and will contribute to the improvement of the proposed system’s ordering workflow, features, and role-based access. 

**Observations.** The researchers will directly observe the ordering procedures being followed by the Finance Department, PSAS Department, student organizations, and students, particularly during high-demand transaction periods, such as event weeks and school enrollment. The observations will focus on the student ordering procedures, payment validation for event merchandise, and order transaction processing of both merchandise and uniform, and uniform inventory management. This will provide real-time understanding of the root causes of student inefficiencies in the ordering process and user behavior, informing system design decisions. 

The documentation of the problem areas with student inefficiencies ordering process, such the lost records, delayed transaction validation, order claiming and product availability follow-ups, the researchers will be able to identify the areas where the current ordering system of the students could be enhanced with inventory automation to reduce frequent product availability checking, and further improve the student’s ordering experience through the unified ordering system. 

**Survey Questionnaires.** In addition to the survey questionnaires will also be applied and distributed to the student stratum to effectively assess the proposed unified ordering system and collect quantifiable data based on the UTAUT Theoretical Framework. This instrument will measure the effectiveness, usefulness of the proposed system in terms of providing an efficient ordering system of the students across the different departments and organizations handling the school uniform and event merchandise ordering process. The feedback gathered from the survey questionnaires will provide comprehensive data collection to support the implementation of the proposed system. 

**3.4 Data Gathering Procedure**

The first step in developing the Unified Ordering System is to observe the student ordering inefficiencies and collect the ordering procedures of school uniforms and event merchandise from the departments and student organizations. The procedures with an in-depth interview with the stakeholders who handle the ordering process of school uniforms and event merchandise, specifically the Finance and PSAS Department, along with student organizations AIM, BAB, JSWAP, and JPIA. The researchers aim to understand the current ordering processes of each department and student organizations, what inefficiencies are encountered by the students in each, and how the current ordering system contributes to these.  

**Preparation.** The team would hold an internal meeting to discuss and assess, based on their observations, the challenges or inefficiencies in the ordering process of the school uniform and event-based merchandise orders. The team would devise a series of focused questions for understanding the current ordering system used in the initial interview with stakeholders.   

**Letter of Invitation.** The researchers will write a letter of invitation to each department and student organization stakeholder. It will then be sent to them through email, followed by a hard copy of the interview request to schedule for their interview availability. The letter of invitation will contain some of the essential information, which includes the introduction of the researchers, a brief description of the research project, the objective of the interview, and the anticipated availability of the interviewees.

**Performing the Interview.** Once the stakeholders set up a schedule based on the researchers' provided availability, the interview will then be conducted in person at the interviewee's preferred location within the La Verdad Christian College. In consideration of their other tasks, the researchers will set the interview duration at an average of 15-30 minutes. During the interview, an audio recording will be used (with the interviewee’s permission) for a more accurate documentation of the interview process.

**Interview Review & Analysis.** After the interview, the research team meets with the group to review and analyze the data collected in the interview. If any points need clarification, the team will schedule a follow-up meeting with stakeholders or reach out to collect the detailed information.

The researchers will administer a survey questionnaire as part of the system assessment and evaluation. The following techniques will be used to collect the data using a structured survey questionnaire and assess the system utilizing quantitative data analysis:

**Preparation.** The researchers will create a standardized survey questionnaire using the items based on the four main factors of the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT), namely the Performance Expectancy, Effort Expectancy, Social Influence, and Facilitating Conditions. 

**Inform the Respondents.** The respondents, especially the proxy respondents from pre-kindergarten and elementary, will be contacted for voluntary participation of proxy respondents in the survey questionnaire and system use testing. The respondents will be notified in advance about the upcoming survey, providing sufficient time to prepare for participating in the system evaluation.

**Conduct the Survey.**  The data from the respondents will be collected through the online platform using Google Forms, well-known for being user-friendly and easily accessible. The survey may be given to them after they have had a chance to use the system extensively. A specific period will be given for completing the questionnaire to ensure comprehensive responses.

**Data Analysis.** After the survey is completed, the collected data will be analyzed using a combination of quantitative methods, specifically descriptive statistics, to evaluate the system's effectiveness and user acceptance.

**3.5  Data Processing and Statistical Treatment**

This section describes how the data collected for the study will be prepared, organized, and statistically treated. This process will ensure accuracy in data interpretation and alignment with the theoretical framework guiding the research.

**3.5.1 Data Processing**

After the data collection phase, the data will be reviewed and analyzed. Google Forms will be used to collect responses from the students. The data collected during the interviews with the Finance Department, the PSAS Department, and the student organization will be reviewed and consolidated using themes for easier statistical analysis. The current manual workflow, including the school uniform inventory management, the student ordering process, and event-based merchandise order management, will be analyzed following the steps in conducting workflow analysis utilized by Staras et al. (2021).

The workflow analysis will be able to help the researchers pinpoint the bottlenecks and inefficiencies encountered by the students in the current ordering process. After conducting the workflow analysis, researchers can easily familiarize themselves with the data and follow Braun and Clarke’s (2006) six phases of thematic analysis. The data will be easier to transcribe and clean, helping the researchers fully absorb the information and identify repeated patterns in the current ordering processes. The data collected will be grouped based on themes, and the responses will be encoded using numerical codes for easier data grouping and tallying. The researchers will tabulate and segment each response, and assign descriptive labels to each code that are related to the research question being addressed in the study. 

The encoded entry will be categorized based on the identified themes to represent persistent issues or problems. This will help the researchers to analyze and count the frequency of problems or issues being encountered by the different stakeholders in the current manual inventory of school uniforms, student order processing, and event-based merchandise order management. Following the Thematic Analysis utilized by Braun and Clarke (2003), researchers can easily organize systematically and represent the data appropriately. With a defined step-by-step process of analyzing the qualitative data, it will guarantee accurate data, removing inconsistencies and redundancies for refutable data analysis.

The data collected from the survey of the students will be cleaned, analyzed, and converted to a tabular format for easier statistical and thematic analysis. The UTAUT (Venkatesh et al., 2003\) will be utilized to examine the influence of acceptance factors on the behavioral intention and user behaviour of the unified ordering system across different departments and student organizations at La Verdad, Apalit Campus. This framework will help researchers perceive the actual use of the proposed system by analyzing the Performance Expectancy, Effort Expectancy, Social Influence, and Facilitating Conditions. The UTAUT framework will help guide the researchers in analyzing the effectiveness and acceptability of the proposed system across the different stakeholder departments and students at La Verdad, Apalit Campus.

The data will then be consolidated and create a thorough analysis by providing quantifiable results and significant insights that will guide the creation and assessment of the proposed system. Tables will be utilized to systematically organize quantitative and qualitative data, and ensure that key patterns, themes, and insights are clearly identified and aligned with the study’s research questions and objectives. The application of thematic analysis and workflow mapping provides structured guidance for interpreting stakeholder feedback, with the integration of the UTAUT framework supporting a theoretical grounding in assessing user behavior and system acceptance.

**3.5.2 Statistical Treatment**

The statistical analysis gives meaning to the meaningless numbers, thereby breathing life into a lifeless data (Ali & Bhaskar, 2016). This is an essential part when it comes to extracting insights from the collected and organized data. Statistical treatment involves statistical methods, such as formulas and tools, in order to analyze and transform raw data to draw patterns, provide meaningful insights, and support conclusions. The researchers will utilize descriptive statistical tools to ensure rigorous data analysis. 

**Descriptive Statistics.** This will be employed to summarize survey responses per construct. Frequency counts and percentages will be used to visually describe data distribution. This will help the researchers and readers understand how often the values occur and their proportion in the datasets. The researchers will also utilize a 4-point Likert scale in measuring the Performance Expectancy (PE), Effort Expectancy (EE), Social Influence (SI), Facilitating Conditions (FC), Behavioral Intention (BI), Usage Behavior (UB), Perceived Usefulness (PU), and Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU) providing ordinal response option (e.g., Strongly Agree, Agree, Neutral, Disagree, Strongly Disagree). These methods will enable the researchers to test the proposed system’s acceptance and usability relevant to the guiding theoretical frameworks.

Mean is utilized in the study to solve for the most common or average responses and interpret the general sentiment and perception of the respondents. The mean will serve as a statistical tool summarizing the gathered responses through the Likert scale-based survey. Understanding the variability in the responses is essential in assessing the reliability and validity of the perception of the proposed system.
